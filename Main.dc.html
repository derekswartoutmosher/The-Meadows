About the Event
The inaugural invitational at The Meadows — the first tournament of its kind, held in the yard of 845 Sciomeadow Drive. Expect a light-hearted, competitive afternoon on a course built from years of overgrowth transformed by a couple shovels, a rake, a new mowing pattern, and a few strategically placed buckets. What started as a backyard dream is becoming something unique — come experience it for yourself — and if you have what it takes, walk away as a champion.

Golf Guide
Six holes, one club, and just enough pressure to make a two-footer feel like a major championship.
Come to be present. Leave with a story.
Welcome to The Meadows
A space where focus and relaxation intersect. Where you can be present, play your game, and still be a decent human being about it — mostly. Simple, but elevated. Inclusive, yet highly competitive. Judgment-free, but filled with good banter.
That’s the whole idea. Everything below just explains how to play it.
Play Over Perfection
The scorecard matters. It’s just not the point. The Meadows isn’t built to protect your handicap or groove the technically correct swing — it’s built for the shot you didn’t know you had in you. Curve one around the tree because you can. Try the flop shot over the woodpile just to see. Bump it off the house if that’s the line you see.
Nobody’s grading your form. Nobody’s checking your setup against a textbook. If it works, you’re a genius. If it doesn’t, you’ve got a story for the next round. Out here, creativity counts for more than a clean stroke — this is a place to learn the game by playing it, not by perfecting it.

Six holes, no repeats, no shortcuts, no “that one doesn’t count.” The back 3 is the stretch where the pressure mounts and the course defends itself.

Equipment: One Club, One Choice
You get one club for the entire round — gap wedge, PW, 9-iron, or 8-iron. No swapping mid-round, no “just for this one hole.” More loft gets you more spin and a shot at being a magician around the hole, less loft trades that touch for raw distance.
Gap Wedge
All spin, all touch, all drama. Less distance off the tee, but around the hole you get to check it up and work it soft while everyone else watches. High-risk ego, high-reward highlight reel.
Pitching Wedge
The middle child. Not flashy, not embarrassing. Shows up, does the job, never gives you a story to tell at the next round.
9-Iron
Trading finesse for distance. Less spin means less control once the ball lands — great for putting real yardage behind a swing on the long holes, less great when you’re trying to finesse it stiff on the par-3s.
8-Iron
Maximum distance, minimum mercy. Strike it clean and you look like a hero. Don’t, and the whole yard knows.
A note on the ball
It’s foam. Feather-light, zero excuses. If it goes sideways, that one’s on the swing.
How to Play
The Invitational has money on the line, so we’re playing it closer to real golf. The course is marked, hazards are defined, and you play it as it lies. Nobody’s allowed to judge a fluffed shot — but the penalty strokes are real.
FairwayPlay it clean. No debate, no drama.
Overgrown AreasMarked on the course. Play it as it lies, or take an unplayable.
PatioMarked on the course. Play it as it lies, or take an unplayable.
BushesTry to hit it out, or take an unplayable. Your call, your stroke count.
TreesNo heroics. Take an unplayable.
UnplayableOne-stroke penalty. Drop within one club length of where it lay, no closer to the hole.
GimmesInside the grip. Pick it up. Nobody’s three-putting a two-incher for pride.
Otherwise — count every swing. Stroke play sets the bracket, then it’s match play to the final.
Etiquette & Banter
Trash talk: encouraged, expected, part of the experience.
Judging someone’s swing, club choice, or first-timer nerves: not allowed.
A scratch player and someone holding a wedge for the first time can play the same round and both have a great time — one of them will just have more material to work with afterward.
Scorecard & Standings
Every stroke counted. Every score on the record. And the record absolutely will be brought up mid-round if you’re losing.
Course Care
Treat the holes, the yard, and the equipment like you want to play here again next weekend — because you will.
Return your club to the rack. Return the balls to the bucket. Return your ego to a reasonable size.
See you at The Meadows.
